Evidence and its limits

Read for legibility, spacing, contextual guidance, stationary targets and access

Designing Better Games: The Next Iteration. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-642-02774-1_60.
Bibliographic/abstract background, not a claim to have read the paywalled full

These are research background and industry guidance, not evidence that this
particular interface has been validated in a controlled human study. The release
uses heuristic review plus automated functional/layout checks. Mattias's actual

Applied decisions

1. Building and crafting share one master/detail layout: personal inventory,
   recipe list, selected recipe details. The list does not cram a large picture,
   long ingredient paragraph and action button into the same space.
2. Every recipe has an icon plus a text label. Readiness has a symbol and text;
   colour alone is not required to understand whether crafting is possible.
3. Basic recipes have a stable order near the beginning. Lists do not reorder
   themselves every time stock changes. Categories reduce the visible choices.
4. Left stick/D-pad moves focus, A acts, B returns, LB/RB switches tabs, LT/RT
   switches recipe categories. Keyboard arrows/Enter/Escape and brackets work;
   PageUp/PageDown changes categories. No mouse-style cursor or dragging is
   required inside these menus. Right stick scrolls recipe/skill details.
5. Unavailable recipes remain focusable, with requirements in the details panel.
   The same validation blocks mouse and controller activation without spending
   materials. No craft occurs just because focus or hover changes.
6. Personal stock stays visible beside recipes at tested desktop/TV sizes.
   Ingredients report both backpack and nearby-chest amounts. On narrow mobile
   widths, the auxiliary inventory column is hidden; the Backpack tab remains.
7. Focus and recipe-list scroll survive tab changes and successful crafting.
   Repeated crafting does not force the player back to the first item.
8. World building retains the absolute tile cursor from 0.4.3. Right stick/D-pad
   changes a tile; walking does not move it; mouse leftovers do not override it.
9. Skill branches are compact vertical groups beside a persistent explanation
   and learn action, rather than large irregular child cards. A learns the
   selected skill; locked skills can still be inspected.
10. Workbench rows have explicit minimum grid track sizes and content-based
    growth. SVG art has bounded dimensions. Automated tests compare text/art
    bounds with their button and detect intersecting rows/panels.

Validation targets

English and Estonian; 1280x720, 1920x1080 and 3840x2160; every listed recipe,
including unavailable recipes; controller-only categories, tab changes and
building; ingredient preservation when crafting is blocked; nearby chest use;
skill focus/learning; natural fresh-game progression and save/reload.

The menu latency test measures skill-menu focus, not every action in the game.
In the recorded successful run: 14, 88, 78, 87, 74, 68 ms. Paused callback work
was about 0.0–0.1 ms. This is one managed Chromium environment, not a hardware
Xbox performance benchmark.

World/rendering decisions

The map is 128x96 tiles, four times the previous area. Larger forest, desert and
sea regions coexist with a southern jungle and northeastern snowy mountains.
The terrain renderer caches visible 16x16-tile chunks with a 64-entry bound,
rather than creating four full-world image layers. Original camp resource IDs
remain stable; expansion resources have independent IDs and mixed coordinate
hashes so they do not form accidental rows. NPC areas stay visually clear.

The cave is farther away and absent from the map until discovered. Karjon gives
a regional clue without requiring completion of his delivery quest. Existing
saves preserve camp buildings, stock and NPC quest flags; old coastal boats are
moved into the expanded sea. Previously found crystals preserve cave discovery.

Rare rainbows use an approximately 8% deterministic daily eligibility check from
day two onward, after 90 active seconds of daylight. An event lasts 120 active
seconds. Its accessible land endpoint is near the player, and can give three
gold once. Event, expiry and claimed state persist in saves. Gold is used in a
buildable golden lantern. The weather test starts just before the trigger on an
eligible day and actually walks to the endpoint; it does not inject a rainbow.
